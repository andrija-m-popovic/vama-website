For applicants

Your attorney owns the petition. We build the business evidence behind it.

Most of our immigration work arrives through counsel. If you found us first, here is how to bring us in the right way - and what to expect.

Discuss a matter Send this page to your attorney

  1. Talk to your attorney first

    Counsel decides the visa category and what evidence the petition needs. That decision shapes every document we prepare.

  2. Bring us in

    Your attorney can contact us directly, or you can start the conversation yourself and tell us who your counsel is.

  3. We coordinate the documents

    We agree the scope with you and your attorney, gather the facts from you once, and send drafts for counsel's review.

What we do

  • Turn your business idea, investment, and plans into a structured business plan
  • Build the financial model, staffing plan, and org charts
  • Research your market with named, dated sources
  • Estimate economic impact with a disclosed method, where counsel asks for it
  • Write down every assumption so you and your attorney can check it

What we never do

  • Tell you whether you qualify for a visa, or which one to choose
  • Promise approval, processing times, or any immigration outcome
  • Write the petition letter or file anything with USCIS
  • Invent numbers to make a plan look stronger - projections come from your facts and stated assumptions

What to have ready

You don't need everything. Rough answers are fine at this stage - we will send a precise checklist once the scope is agreed.

Useful now

  • The visa category your attorney is pursuing
  • Your attorney's name and firm
  • What the business does, where, and for whom
  • The planned investment and roughly what it buys
  • Hiring plans, even if rough
  • Your filing or response date

Please don't send yet

  • Passports, visas, or identity documents
  • Bank statements or source-of-funds records
  • Tax returns or payroll files
  • Prior petitions or USCIS notices

We ask for exactly what the scope needs, through a secure exchange, after you accept the proposal.

Before any scope: a CV review at no charge

If you are unsure what business evidence your case will need, send us your CV and the category your attorney is pursuing. We read it against the evidence that category usually needs and reply with what we would document and where the record looks thin.

What you get back

  • A short written note: which documents your facts would support, and which gaps we would expect counsel to ask about
  • The order we would prepare them in, so you and your attorney can decide what to commission
  • Nothing filed, nothing promised

What it is not

  • Not an eligibility opinion. We do not say whether you qualify, and we do not pick the category. Your attorney does.
  • Not a sales step in disguise. If the documents are not worth commissioning yet, we say so.
Email your CV for review

Send the CV only: no passports, bank statements or filings. You hear back within 1 business day.

How every engagement is bounded

VAMA is not a law firm. We prepare business analysis and documents; counsel keeps legal strategy, eligibility decisions, petition arguments, and filing. Projections are planning estimates, not achieved results, and nothing we write is an assurance of approval.

The accepted proposal defines the documents, review rounds, formats, and timing. Changed facts, later RFE work, or new documents are quoted as a written scope extension - never as a surprise invoice.

Have a matter with a date on it?

Send the visa category, the documents you need, and the deadline. Within 1 business day you get a yes, a no, or up to 5 clarifying questions - then a written proposal with a fixed fee. No confidential files until we agree a secure exchange.
