What the documents need to make clear

Counsel preparing L-1A or L-1B petitions, including new-office filings and extensions.

The usual trouble spot: five documents written at different times, so the headcount in the org chart is not the headcount in the payroll schedule.

Counsel decides what the petition argues. Our job is to make the business facts behind it specific, sourced, and consistent from the plan to the model to the org chart.

The relationship, in business termsOwnership, parent operations, and where the U.S. entity sits in the group.
Managerial or executive structureWho reports to whom now, and in the Year 1 and Year 5 projections.
Operating substancePremises, customers, contracts, and the first-year build-out of the U.S. office.
FundingHow the U.S. office is financed until it supports itself.

What we need to scope it

  • Parent company profile, ownership structure, and a financial summary
  • U.S. entity status: formed, office secured, bank account open
  • The transferee's role and who will report to them
  • Planned U.S. hires and timing
  • Customers or contracts in place or in negotiation

What not to send yet

Corporate records, tax returns, payroll files, and passports. We list exactly what the scope needs after the proposal is accepted and a secure exchange is set up.

How every engagement is bounded

VAMA is not a law firm. We prepare business analysis and documents; counsel keeps legal strategy, eligibility decisions, petition arguments, and filing. Projections are planning estimates, not achieved results, and nothing we write is an assurance of approval.

The accepted proposal defines the documents, review rounds, formats, and timing. Changed facts, later RFE work, or new documents are quoted as a written scope extension - never as a surprise invoice.

L-1 questions counsel asks us

Can you prepare the L-1 and a later EB-1C plan as one case family?

Yes. Building both from one source of truth keeps the org charts, headcount, and financials consistent between filings. The outdoor-equipment distribution case in our library is an example.

Do you describe the transferee's duties?

We describe the role inside the business plan and org charts: reporting lines, direct reports, and the decisions the role owns. How duties are characterized for legal purposes is counsel's call.

Is an extension plan different from a new-office plan?

Yes. An extension plan compares what was projected with what happened, explains the differences, and updates the forward plan. Share the prior plan when we scope.
