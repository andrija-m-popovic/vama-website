Assumptions log included

You know a file is in trouble when the feedback arrives in pieces.

The client changes the staffing. The attorney wants the projections more aggressive. The agency wants them more conservative. Each change arrives separately, and each one ripples through the plan, the model and the org chart.

Multiple plan versions and an uncertain filed baseline.

Figures that no longer match between exhibits.

"Document received" treated as "reviewed and usable".

The three patterns we see most often in files that come to us for repair. The practice is built to prevent them.

Locked inputs. One consolidated round. A package that agrees with itself.

The method is simple and it is not negotiable, because it is what keeps the plan, the model and the org chart consistent when the file is read side by side.

  1. Intake and analysis

    We read the source documents first and raise every inconsistency before a model exists. Missing inputs are named before they become hidden assumptions.

  2. The numbers are locked

    Once the client and counsel confirm the inputs, that is the baseline. Later changes are a written scope extension, so counsel always knows what changed and why.

  3. Draft, then one consolidated round

    Client and counsel agree on what comes back to us. Contradictory fragments are not accepted; they are reconciled first.

  4. Handover with the workings

    An editable model, a sources list with dates, and an assumptions log. Counsel can test any number without calling us.

How every engagement is bounded

VAMA is not a law firm. We prepare business analysis and documents; counsel keeps legal strategy, eligibility decisions, petition arguments, and filing. Projections are planning estimates, not achieved results, and nothing we write is an assurance of approval.

The accepted proposal defines the documents, review rounds, formats, and timing. Changed facts, later RFE work, or new documents are quoted as a written scope extension - never as a surprise invoice.

Questions counsel asks first

Do you give legal advice or file petitions?

No. VAMA is not a law firm. We prepare business documents and analysis. The petition letter is your work; the exhibits behind it are ours.

Can you work white-label under our firm's name?

Yes. We prepare documents behind the scenes under your process and communication rhythm, branded or unbranded. Referred relationships stay with your firm; we never go around you. Start with one scoped pilot matter before any volume commitment.

Do you work directly with applicants?

Yes, alongside their attorney. We agree the business-document scope with the client and counsel, and counsel keeps legal strategy. We don't assess eligibility or promise outcomes.

What if the client's numbers change after the draft?

Changes that arrive in the consolidated review round are handled in it. After that, we quote the update as a small written scope extension. Locked inputs are what keep the exhibits consistent, and they are also what keep the fee fixed.

How do you handle confidential files?

Nothing confidential goes through the website. After the proposal is accepted, we set up a secure exchange and send a checklist of exactly what the scope needs. Client data is placed in an AI tool only under an approved data mode, and every deliverable is reviewed by a person.

What method do your economic impact studies use?

A BLS-based Type I input-output method: direct plus indirect effects, reported as output and job-years, with sources, boundaries and limits stated. We do not report induced effects. If a matter requires a specific licensed model, we say so at scope.

What if the case receives an RFE later?

RFE work is a new scope: an audit and issue matrix first, then targeted repair. If we prepared the original documents, the audit is faster because the source of truth already exists.

Have a matter with a date on it?

Send the visa category, the documents you need, and the deadline. Within 1 business day you get a yes, a no, or up to 5 clarifying questions - then a written proposal with a fixed fee. No confidential files until we agree a secure exchange.
