What the documents need to make clear

Counsel preparing EB-1C petitions, often after an L-1 filing for the same executive.

The usual trouble spot: an L-1 plan from another provider whose numbers no longer match what actually happened in the U.S. entity.

Counsel decides what the petition argues. Our job is to make the business facts behind it specific, sourced, and consistent from the plan to the model to the org chart.

What we need to scope it

  • Current org charts for both entities - role titles are enough at this stage
  • A financial summary for each entity
  • A description of the executive's decisions and direct reports
  • A list of U.S. operating evidence (contracts, sales) - the list, not the documents
  • The expansion plan for the next 3-5 years

What not to send yet

Payroll records, tax filings, and contracts. We request them against a checklist after the proposal is accepted.

Timeline and revisions

Timing is fixed in the proposal against your filing date. Revisions within the agreed scope are included for 3 months after the first draft.

How every engagement is bounded

VAMA is not a law firm. We prepare business analysis and documents; counsel keeps legal strategy, eligibility decisions, petition arguments, and filing. Projections are planning estimates, not achieved results, and nothing we write is an assurance of approval.

The accepted proposal defines the documents, review rounds, formats, and timing. Changed facts, later RFE work, or new documents are quoted as a written scope extension - never as a surprise invoice.

EB-1C questions counsel asks us

The L-1 was prepared by another provider. Can you build the EB-1C plan?

Yes. We start from the L-1 plan and what has happened since, and flag any numbers that no longer line up before counsel sees the draft.

How detailed are the org charts?

Detailed enough to show the layers: the executive, the managers, and the staff who do the work, with titles and headcount per unit, for both entities.

Do you decide whether the role qualifies as executive or managerial?

No. We document the business facts clearly. Counsel decides how they are characterized and argued.
