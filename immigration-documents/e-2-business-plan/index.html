Who this page is for

What the documents need to make clear

Counsel preparing an E-2 treaty investor filing, and investors whose attorney has asked for a business plan.

Counsel decides what the petition argues. Our job is to make the business facts behind it specific, sourced, and consistent from the plan to the model to the org chart.

Investment deployment

What the committed funds buy, when, and why that amount fits the enterprise.

Viability

A revenue build tied to real pricing, capacity, and local market data - not a growth percentage.

Non-marginality

Projected payroll and hiring, year by year, beyond the investor's own support.

Operating substance

Location, suppliers, sales channels, and the investor's role in directing the business.

What we need to scope it

  • Investment amount and a draft use-of-funds breakdown
  • What the business sells, to whom, and at what price
  • Location status: lease signed, in negotiation, or still searching
  • Hiring intentions - roles and rough timing are enough
  • For a franchise or acquisition: the franchise disclosure summary or the seller's recent financials
  • The investor's CV or a short role summary

What not to send yet

Bank statements, source-of-funds records, passports, and prior filings. We ask for exactly what the scope needs, through a secure exchange, after the proposal is accepted.

Timeline and revisions

Timing is fixed in the proposal against your filing date. Our standard path once intake is complete: evidence intake on days 1-3, draft and internal QA on days 4-7, counsel review and a clean final package by day 10. One consolidated revision round is included.

How every engagement is bounded

VAMA is not a law firm. We prepare business analysis and documents; counsel keeps legal strategy, eligibility decisions, petition arguments, and filing. Projections are planning estimates, not achieved results, and nothing we write is an assurance of approval.

The accepted proposal defines the documents, review rounds, formats, and timing. Changed facts, later RFE work, or new documents are quoted as a written scope extension - never as a surprise invoice.

E-2 questions counsel asks us

Do you prepare the source-of-funds section?

We can build the business side of it: a clear framework and timeline assembled from the records the client provides. Counsel decides how source of funds is argued and which records are filed.

Can the plan cover a franchise or an acquisition?

Yes. For a franchise we build from the franchisor's disclosure data and the investor's territory. For an acquisition we start from the seller's historical financials and show what changes under new ownership.

What happens if the client's numbers change after the draft?

Changes that arrive before the revision round are handled in it. After that, we quote the update as a small written scope extension, so counsel always knows what changed and why.
