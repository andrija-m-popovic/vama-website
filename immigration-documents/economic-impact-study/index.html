Deliverable · Economic Impact Study

Economic impact studies with the method on the page

Estimated output and job-years from the endeavor or enterprise, built with a disclosed input-output method - so counsel can explain every number, and a reviewer can follow how it was reached.

Discuss a matter Read the method

Most often used in EB-2 NIW and EB-5 matters, and in other high-value filings where economic contribution matters.

BLS-based Type I input-output analysis

We start from the plan's direct spending, revenue, and hiring, then apply published U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics input-output data to estimate the indirect effect on supplier industries.

  • Direct effects: the enterprise's own output and jobs, taken from the business plan and financial model
  • Indirect effects: output and jobs in supplier industries, from BLS industry multipliers matched to the enterprise's industry code
  • Reported as: total output and job-years over the plan period, by year
  • Not reported: induced (household spending) effects. Leaving them out keeps the estimate conservative and easier to defend

If your matter needs a specific licensed model

Some matters call for a particular regional model such as RIMS II or IMPLAN. Tell us at scope. We will say plainly whether we can meet that requirement before anyone commits, rather than relabel our method.

What we need to scope it

  • The business plan and financial model, or a draft of each
  • The enterprise's location and industry
  • The hiring plan by year
  • The filing date

If we are writing the plan too

The study is built from the same model, so every direct figure in the study matches the plan exactly. This is the most common and most consistent setup.

If someone else wrote the plan

We check the plan's figures first and flag any that cannot support an impact estimate before we build on them.

How every engagement is bounded

Impact study questions

Are the results a forecast of what will happen?

No. They are planning estimates derived from the plan's assumptions and the stated method. The study says so, and shows which inputs drive the result.

Why not include induced effects?

Induced effects depend on assumptions about household spending that are harder to defend for a single new enterprise. Excluding them gives a smaller but more conservative number.

Can you do the study on its own?

Yes, as long as the underlying plan and model can support it. We review them first and tell you if anything needs fixing before the study is worth building.
