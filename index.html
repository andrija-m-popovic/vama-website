  1. Quote follow-up at Company A, a fictional 11-person HVAC and plumbing company.

    The office manager emails the quote. Nobody owns the second touch. The customer goes quiet.

    Illustrative. See the full sample audit.

  2. What we examined, and how each fact is labeled.

    • Observed: 41% of open quotes had no second touch within 7 days (90-day quote list, redacted).
    • Observed: gross profit per job about $1,500 (job records).
    • Reported, not yet validated: "we probably lose 1 in 10" (owner interview).

  3. Compared on the same problem, in this order.

    • Process change: a named owner and a 3-touch cadence. Low effort, high impact.
    • Existing software: reminders already included in the field-service system. Low effort.
    • Automation: an automatic follow-up email after 3 days. Low to medium.
    • AI: drafted follow-ups the office approves. Medium; only after the notes are structured.

  4. The roadmap names the smallest change worth testing first.

    Turn on the reminders you already pay for, name the owner, run it for 30 days. Measure the second-touch rate: baseline 59%, target 90%. No implementation purchase needed. If the close rate moves, consider automation next.

From the question to a document you can review.

The same four steps in both practices. The examples come from a cleared financial-planning case for a vehicle-rental business.

  1. Question

    Define the decision the work has to support.

    "What level of demand justifies another vehicle purchase?"

  2. Evidence

    Name the sources and inputs, and what is missing, before anything is modeled.

    Utilization, fleet cost and maintenance history, staged around demand.

  3. Analysis

    Connect the narrative, the model and the operating plan, then test the downside.

    Purchases pause if utilization weakens; reserves respond to a maintenance-cost increase.

  4. Usable output

    An editable model, a sources list and an assumptions log the reader can check.

    A driver-and-control framework behind the projections, with scenario responses.
