Connecting healthcare-technology research to a proposal and pitch

Market research, proposal documentation and a pitch presentation for a healthcare-technology project, built so the wider market and the selected regional markets could be examined separately.

Identifying details and confidential figures are omitted. This case describes research and documentation work. It does not establish clinical effectiveness, regulatory authorization, funding, customer adoption or commercial success.

The question

A healthcare-technology project required market research, proposal documentation and a pitch presentation. The assignment extended beyond describing the technology: it had to examine the market in which the proposed solution would operate and the users it was intended to serve.

The constraints

A global market description is not a commercial strategy. The research therefore covered the broader market and a closer examination of selected regional markets, so the work could address both the wider opportunity and the conditions relevant to the proposed route to market.

The evidence examined

  • Market context: the relevant healthcare-technology and device markets, with regional market analysis.
  • Potential users: target-user profiles and the market challenges associated with the intended use of the solution.
  • Competing offerings: competitor analysis to place the proposed solution alongside existing alternatives.
  • Routes to market: a go-to-market strategy connecting the research with commercial-planning considerations.

The decisions made in the documents

The outputs were kept distinct because they served different purposes. The research report provided market and competitor context. The proposal documentation framed the project. The pitch presentation communicated the proposed solution, its intended users and the market challenges. Together they gave the team complementary materials for examining and explaining the opportunity, without letting one document carry claims that belonged in another.

The deliverables

  • A market-research report covering the wider market, regional analysis, competitors and go-to-market strategy.
  • Project-proposal documentation.
  • A pitch presentation connecting the project to its intended users and market context.

What the reader can check

The questions a product description alone cannot answer: who is the proposed solution for? What alternatives already exist? Which market conditions deserve closer attention? How should the opportunity be explained to a reviewer or partner? The example shows the scope and the relationship of the prepared materials; it does not present a specific market finding or product-performance claim as independently validated. A new engagement would define its research questions, markets, sources and required outputs before work begins.
